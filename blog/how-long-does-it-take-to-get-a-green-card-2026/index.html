How Long Does It Take to Get a Green Card in 2026?

October 20, 2026

One of the most common questions immigrants and their families ask is simple: How long does it take to get a Green Card in 2026?

Unfortunately, there is no single answer.

Depending on how you qualify for permanent residence, where you are applying from, your relationship to the petitioner, your country of chargeability, visa availability, USCIS processing times, and whether additional evidence is requested, the process could take months — or, in some immigration categories, many years.

Understanding the Green Card processing time in 2026 is especially important because two applicants who submit paperwork around the same time may have completely different timelines.

For example, the spouse of a U.S. citizen may have an immigrant visa immediately available, while the sibling of a U.S. citizen may have to wait years for a visa number to become available.

This guide explains the 2026 Green Card timeline, including marriage-based Green Cards, family-based immigration, adjustment of status, consular processing, Form I-130, Form I-485, priority dates, the Visa Bulletin, interviews, Requests for Evidence, and common factors that can delay a case.

What Is the Average Green Card Processing Time in 2026?

There is no universal average processing time that applies to every Green Card application.

USCIS publishes processing information by form, immigration category, and, where applicable, processing location. Processing times also change as USCIS workloads and case volumes change.

According to USCIS historical national processing data through June 30, 2026, the median processing time for Form I-130, Petition for Alien Relative, for an immediate relative was approximately 12.9 months during fiscal year 2026.

However, that figure should not be interpreted as meaning that every family-based Green Card takes approximately 13 months.

Form I-130 is only one part of many Green Card cases.

An applicant may also need to complete:

  • Form I-485, Application to Register Permanent Residence or Adjust Status
  • biometrics
  • a medical examination
  • financial sponsorship documentation
  • background and security checks
  • an immigration interview
  • National Visa Center processing
  • consular processing
  • additional evidence requested by USCIS or the U.S. Department of State

Additionally, immigrants in preference categories may have to wait until an immigrant visa becomes available.

Therefore, when asking "How long will my Green Card take?", the first question should really be: what type of Green Card case do you have?

Marriage Green Card Processing Time in 2026

Marriage continues to be one of the most common paths to lawful permanent residence.

However, the timeline depends heavily on whether the petitioning spouse is a U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident and whether the foreign spouse is currently inside or outside the United States.

Marriage to a U.S. Citizen

The spouse of a U.S. citizen is classified as an immediate relative under U.S. immigration law.

This distinction is extremely important.

Immediate relatives of U.S. citizens are not subject to the annual numerical limits that apply to many other family preference categories. Therefore, an immigrant visa is generally immediately available to an otherwise eligible spouse.

That does not mean USCIS will approve the Green Card immediately. The applicant must still complete the required immigration process.

Example: U.S. Citizen Spouse and Adjustment of Status

Consider this hypothetical example.

Maria entered the United States lawfully and later married David, a U.S. citizen.

Assuming Maria is otherwise eligible to adjust status, the couple may be able to submit Form I-130 and Form I-485 concurrently, along with the other required documentation.

Their application package may include evidence such as:

  • marriage certificate
  • proof of David's U.S. citizenship
  • proof of Maria's lawful admission
  • financial sponsorship documentation
  • medical examination
  • joint bank statements
  • joint lease or mortgage documents
  • insurance records
  • photographs together
  • other evidence demonstrating a bona fide marriage

Because Maria is the spouse of a U.S. citizen, she does not have to wait for a family-preference priority date to become current before a visa can be issued.

Her actual processing time, however, will depend on USCIS's handling of the case, the applicable field office, background checks, whether an interview is required, and whether USCIS requests additional documentation.

What Is Concurrent Filing?

Concurrent filing can significantly affect the practical timeline of some Green Card cases.

When an immigrant visa is immediately available, and the applicant is otherwise eligible, certain applicants can file the immigrant petition and adjustment-of-status application at the same time.

In a marriage case involving a U.S. citizen, for example, the couple may be able to submit Form I-130, Petition for Alien Relative and Form I-485, Application to Register Permanent Residence or Adjust Status together.

USCIS specifically advises applicants who concurrently filed an I-485 with an I-130, I-140, or I-360 to look at the applicable I-485 processing time when evaluating how long the combined case may take.

How Long Does Form I-130 Take in 2026?

Form I-130 establishes a qualifying family relationship between the petitioner and beneficiary.

As noted above, USCIS's historical national data for fiscal year 2026 through June 30 showed a median of approximately 12.9 months for immediate-relative I-130 petitions.

But an approved I-130 does not automatically give someone a Green Card.

This is an important misconception.

I-130 approval establishes the qualifying family relationship. The beneficiary must still complete the appropriate process to become a lawful permanent resident.

Depending on the circumstances, that may mean:

  • Adjustment of status through USCIS if the applicant qualifies to complete the process inside the United States; or
  • Consular processing through the Department of State if the applicant will apply for an immigrant visa abroad.

How Long Does Form I-485 Take in 2026?

Form I-485 is the primary application used by eligible applicants seeking adjustment of status to permanent residence from inside the United States.

There is no single nationwide processing time that accurately predicts every I-485 case.

Processing can vary by field office and case type.

USCIS processing-time estimates are also not guarantees. Some applications are completed faster, while others take considerably longer.

An I-485 case may involve:

  • receipt and initial processing
  • biometrics
  • background checks
  • review of supporting documentation
  • medical examination documentation
  • Requests for Evidence
  • interviews, when required
  • final adjudication

This is why comparing your Green Card case to a friend or relative's case can be misleading.

Even applications filed within days of one another can have different timelines.

Green Card Through a Permanent Resident Spouse

The timeline can be different when the petitioning spouse has a Green Card rather than U.S. citizenship.

Spouses and qualifying children of lawful permanent residents generally fall under the F2A family preference category.

Unlike immediate relatives of U.S. citizens, family preference categories are subject to numerical limitations.

This means visa availability becomes an important part of the timeline.

As of the September 2026 Visa Bulletin, the F2A Dates for Filing category was listed as current across the listed chargeability areas. However, Final Action Dates still applied, illustrating why applicants must distinguish between being permitted to move forward with certain filing steps and being eligible for final Green Card approval.

Visa Bulletin dates can move forward, remain unchanged, or retrogress.

Therefore, applicants should not assume that today's Visa Bulletin will remain the same several months from now.

What Is a Priority Date?

For many family- and employment-based Green Card applicants, the priority date determines their place in line for an immigrant visa.

In a family petition, the priority date is generally tied to when USCIS properly receives the petition.

Think of it like taking a number at a very large immigration waiting line.

Having an approved petition does not necessarily mean that the beneficiary can immediately receive permanent residence.

For preference-category immigrants, the priority date generally must become eligible under the applicable Visa Bulletin chart before the case can reach the appropriate stage.

Why Can Some Family Green Cards Take Years?

Congress limits the number of immigrant visas available annually in many family-sponsored categories.

These categories include:

  • F1: Unmarried adult sons and daughters of U.S. citizens
  • F2A: Spouses and qualifying children of permanent residents
  • F2B: Unmarried adult sons and daughters of permanent residents
  • F3: Married sons and daughters of U.S. citizens
  • F4: Brothers and sisters of adult U.S. citizens

The waiting periods can be substantial.

For example, the September 2026 Visa Bulletin showed family-sponsored Final Action Dates reaching back many years in several categories.

For the F4 sibling category, the Final Action Date for most chargeability areas was October 22, 2011. Certain countries had even earlier dates.

This demonstrates why asking for an average Green Card processing time without identifying the immigration category can produce a misleading answer.

An immediate-relative case and a sibling petition are both technically family-based immigration cases, but their potential timelines are dramatically different.

Case Study: Green Card Through a U.S. Citizen Parent

Imagine that Carlos is 19 years old, unmarried, and the child of a U.S. citizen.

Because Carlos is under 21 and unmarried, he may potentially qualify as an immediate relative of a U.S. citizen.

That means there is generally no numerical visa waiting list for his category.

Now change one fact.

Suppose Carlos is 25.

His immigration classification may be different because he is no longer a "child" for purposes of the immediate-relative category.

That single difference can substantially change the Green Card timeline.

This example demonstrates why age, marital status, petitioner status, and family relationship must all be evaluated carefully.

Case Study: Green Card Through a Brother or Sister

Suppose Ana is a U.S. citizen over age 21 and wants to petition for her brother, Roberto.

Ana can potentially file Form I-130 for Roberto.

However, Roberto falls into the F4 sibling category, which is numerically limited.

Even if USCIS approves Ana's I-130 petition, Roberto may still face a lengthy wait before an immigrant visa becomes available.

The September 2026 Visa Bulletin illustrates the extent of these backlogs.

This is why I-130 approval and Green Card approval are not the same thing.

Green Card Through Consular Processing in 2026

If the beneficiary is living outside the United States, the Green Card process will often involve consular processing.

A typical family-based consular case may proceed through several stages.

Step 1: File Form I-130. The U.S. citizen or permanent resident petitioner submits the family petition to USCIS.

Step 2: USCIS reviews the petition. USCIS determines whether the petitioner has established the qualifying family relationship.

Step 3: Case goes to the National Visa Center. After approval, cases requiring consular processing are generally transferred to the National Visa Center when appropriate.

Step 4: NVC documentation. The applicant and petitioner may need to provide financial documents, civil records, the immigrant visa application, and other required information.

Step 5: Visa availability. Preference-category applicants may have to wait for their priority date.

Step 6: Medical examination. The applicant completes the required immigrant medical examination with an authorized physician.

Step 7: Consular interview. The applicant attends an immigrant visa interview at the appropriate U.S. embassy or consulate.

Step 8: Immigrant visa decision. If approved and otherwise admissible, the applicant may receive an immigrant visa.

Step 9: Entering the United States. After admission to the United States with the appropriate immigrant visa, the individual generally becomes a lawful permanent resident.

Each stage can add time to the overall process.

What Can Delay a Green Card Application?

Even a seemingly straightforward case can experience delays.

One of the most common causes is incomplete or inconsistent documentation.

Requests for Evidence. USCIS may issue a Request for Evidence (RFE) when additional documentation is necessary to adjudicate the application. An RFE could involve insufficient financial sponsorship evidence, missing civil documents, incomplete medical documentation, inadequate proof of a bona fide marriage, missing immigration records, or questions about eligibility. Responding improperly — or failing to respond before the deadline — can create serious problems.

Background and security checks. Green Card applicants generally undergo identity, security, and background screening. Some cases require additional review.

Immigration history. Previous immigration violations can make a case more complicated. Issues may include unlawful presence, unauthorized employment, prior removal proceedings, deportation orders, misrepresentation, prior visa denials, overstays, or unlawful entries. Whether a particular issue prevents adjustment or requires a waiver depends on the specific circumstances.

Criminal history. Arrests, criminal charges, and convictions can also affect Green Card eligibility. Applicants should not assume that a dismissed case or old conviction is irrelevant. Immigration law has its own rules regarding criminal conduct, and those rules do not always correspond neatly with state criminal law.

Can You Speed Up a Green Card Application?

There is no general method that guarantees faster Green Card approval.

However, applicants can reduce avoidable delays by preparing the case correctly from the beginning.

That means submitting the correct forms, using current editions, paying the proper fees, including required supporting evidence, providing accurate information, attending appointments, monitoring USCIS notices, and responding appropriately to requests.

USCIS specifically warns that incomplete petitions may be rejected or result in requests for additional evidence or information, which can delay processing.

A carefully prepared application does not guarantee approval or a particular processing time, but it can help prevent unnecessary problems caused by avoidable filing mistakes.

How Do I Check My Green Card Processing Time?

Applicants should rely on the official USCIS Case Processing Times system rather than estimates from friends, social media posts, or old immigration articles.

USCIS updates its processing-time information regularly.

You will generally need information from your receipt notice, including the form type, immigration category, and potentially the office handling the case.

Remember that a published processing time is an estimate, not a promise that your case will be decided by a specific date.

Green Card Processing Time FAQs

How long does a marriage Green Card take in 2026?

There is no universal timeline. The process depends on whether the petitioner is a U.S. citizen or permanent resident, whether the beneficiary is inside or outside the United States, the USCIS office involved, visa availability, and the complexity of the case.

How long does I-130 take in 2026?

USCIS historical national data through June 30, 2026 reported a median processing time of approximately 12.9 months for immediate-relative I-130 petitions. Individual cases may take less or considerably more time.

Does an approved I-130 mean I have a Green Card?

No. I-130 approval does not itself grant lawful permanent residence. It establishes the qualifying family relationship. The beneficiary must still complete adjustment of status or immigrant visa processing, as applicable.

Can a U.S. citizen's spouse get a Green Card faster?

Spouses of U.S. citizens have an important advantage because they are classified as immediate relatives and immigrant visas are not subject to the same numerical waiting lists applicable to family preference categories. However, they must still satisfy all eligibility requirements and complete USCIS or consular processing.

Can USCIS approve a Green Card without an interview?

Some cases may be adjudicated without an interview when USCIS determines one is unnecessary. Other applicants will be required to attend an interview. Applicants should never assume an interview will automatically be waived.

Why is my friend's Green Card taking less time than mine?

No two immigration cases are identical. Different field offices, immigration categories, filing dates, background checks, Requests for Evidence, visa availability, country-specific limitations, and individual immigration histories can produce significantly different timelines.

The Bottom Line: How Long Does It Take to Get a Green Card in 2026?

The Green Card processing time in 2026 depends primarily on how the applicant qualifies for permanent residence.

For an immediate relative of a U.S. citizen, an immigrant visa is generally immediately available, eliminating the multi-year visa-number waiting period that affects many preference categories.

For other family-based applicants, however, the Visa Bulletin may add years to the process.

The key factors include:

  • immigration category
  • petitioner's immigration status
  • applicant's age and marital status
  • adjustment of status versus consular processing
  • USCIS processing times
  • country of chargeability
  • priority date
  • Visa Bulletin movement
  • completeness of the application
  • Requests for Evidence
  • immigration history
  • criminal history
  • whether a waiver or additional immigration filing is necessary

Because the difference between categories can mean the difference between months and years, understanding your immigration classification before filing is essential.
